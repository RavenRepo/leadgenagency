# Newsletter Sequence Skill

Email frameworks (PAS, AIDA, Story-Proof-Offer), cadence strategy, and sequence design for the email-sequence-writer and newsletter-strategist agents.

## Email Frameworks

### PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution)
1. Problem: Name the pain
2. Agitate: Make it worse / more urgent
3. Solution: Introduce your offer

Best for: Cold audiences, addressing a pressing problem

### AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action)
1. Attention: Hook headline
2. Interest: Build curiosity
3. Desire: Paint picture of better state
4. Action: CTA

Best for: Warm audiences ready for solution

### Story-Proof-Offer
1. Story: Relatable narrative (personal or client)
2. Proof: Evidence the story is real/typical
3. Offer: Soft introduction of solution

Best for: Building trust before pitching

## Email Sequence Design

### The 7-Email Welcome Sequence

**Email 1 (D0 — Delivery)**
- Job: Deliver lead magnet, set expectations
- Framework: Direct delivery
- CTA: Access the lead magnet
- PS: Tease email 2 ("Tomorrow I'm sharing...")

**Email 2 (D1 — Quick Win)**
- Job: Deliver immediate insight from lead magnet
- Framework: Value-first
- CTA: Soft mention of paid offer
- Tone: Authoritative, helpful

**Email 3 (D2 — Story)**
- Job: Build human connection
- Framework: Story-Proof-Offer
- CTA: Reply-only (no link)
- Tone: Personal, vulnerable

**Email 4 (D4 — Social Proof)**
- Job: Build credibility and trust
- Framework: Proof-heavy
- CTA: "DM me" or soft offer mention
- PS: Include testimonial

**Email 5 (D6 — Objection)**
- Job: Address #1 objection
- Framework: PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution)
- Format: FAQ or "I hear this a lot..."
- CTA: Soft, non-pushy

**Email 6 (D8 — Value)**
- Job: Pure value drop, build goodwill
- Framework: Educational
- CTA: No pitch, pure generosity
- Tone: Teacher, not seller

**Email 7 (D10 — Offer)**
- Job: Convert to paid customer
- Framework: Direct response
- CTA: Clear, specific, time-limited
- PS: Bonus or urgency element

## Subject Line Formulas

### Pattern 1: Curiosity Gap
- "[Number] years of [topic] taught me this:"
- "I found something strange in my [data/analytics]:"
- "Nobody talks about this, but..."

### Pattern 2: Specific Result
- "How I got [result] in [timeframe]:"
- "The exact [outcome] that [specific person] got:"
- "[Result] without [common approach]:"

### Pattern 3: Direct Statement
- "My honest take on [industry topic]:"
- "Why [common belief] is wrong:"
- "What [successful people/companies] do differently:"

### Subject Line Rules
- Max 8 words
- No clickbait or misleading
- Preview text completes subject, not repeats
- Test different angles: curiosity vs. direct

## Newsletter Cadence

### Weekly Newsletter Structure
- Day: Tuesday or Wednesday (highest open rates)
- Time: 8-10am local (reader's timezone)
- Frequency: Weekly (bi-weekly if struggling for content)

### Issue Template
1. Header: Newsletter name + issue number
2. Section A (400-600 words): One big insight
3. Section B: Resource/tool recommendation
4. Section C: Subscriber win or case study
5. Closing CTA: Rotate lead magnet / offer / reply ask

### Monetization Ladder (60-90 Days)
- Weeks 1-4: Pure value, build trust
- Weeks 5-8: Introduce paid offer via story/case study
- Weeks 9-12: Direct pitch cycle

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Subject lines max 8 words
- [ ] Preview text completes subject
- [ ] One CTA per email
- [ ] No salesy language before email 7
- [ ] Sequence reads as conversation, not campaign
- [ ] Each email has one job
- [ ] PS on emails 1, 4, 7 only
